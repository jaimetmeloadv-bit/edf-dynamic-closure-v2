\begin{abstract}
The Entropic Dimensional Framework (EDF) is a pregeometric proposal in which finite phase resolution and information-reducing projections precede effective geometric dynamics. This Version 2 replaces the previously assumed twelve-sector kernel by a conditional closure derivation. A finite phase resolver with simple spectrum and a transitive cyclic traversal generate the complete matrix algebra $M_k(\C)$, so the dynamic closure capacity is $C(k)=k^2$. Applying a separately stated Fibonacci Compatibility Condition, $F_k=C(k)$, gives $F_k=k^2$; the classification of Fibonacci perfect powers then selects the unique nontrivial solution $k=12$. Nonprimitive traversals are shown to generate the exact block algebra $\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{d}M_{k/d}(\C)$, with $d=\gcd(k,r)$ and capacity $C_r(k)=k^2/d$. For $k=12$, the Chinese remainder theorem further gives $\Z_{12}\cong\Z_3\times\Z_4$ and $\C^{12}\cong\C^3\otimes\C^4$. These results are used to repair the seven theorem statements of EDF: solitonic residues, triplication, twelve-fold informational quantization, braid closure, closure-capacity scaling, ultraviolet convergence, and entropy descent. A stochastic realization separates structural closure from environmental selection, while exact Schur reduction shows that a transient state may disappear from the explicit final description and remain encoded in effective boundary dynamics. The article distinguishes proved conditional mathematics from EDF-to-physics identifications that remain open to empirical test.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Entropic Dimensional Framework (EDF) was introduced as a pregeometric model in which effective geometry and dynamics arise through a hierarchy of information-reducing maps rather than being imposed on an already continuous spacetime \cite{meloV1}. The first formulation organized the proposal around seven named results: soliton generation, soliton triplication, twelve-fold quantization, twelve-crossing confinement, writhe-bounded gravity, ultraviolet finiteness, and an entropic-arrow hierarchy. Subsequent mathematical and computational work showed that several of those statements contained two different logical layers: a mathematically controlled finite-structure problem, and a further identification of that structure with a physical observable. The present revision separates these layers>
\begin{equation}
\text{phase dynamics}
\longrightarrow
\text{operator closure}
\longrightarrow
C(k)
\longrightarrow
\text{compatibility}
\longrightarrow
k,
\end{equation}
rather than a search for consequences of a preselected value.

The finite shift--phase algebra used below belongs to the standard theory of Schwinger and Weyl operators on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces \cite{marchiolli2013,bertlmann2008,vourdas2004}. The arithmetic step uses the theorem of Bugeaud, Mignotte, and Siksek that the only perfect powers in the Fibonacci sequence are $0,1,8,$ and $144$ \cite{bugeaud2006}. The conjunction of these two ingredients is specific to EDF only through the compatibility condition introduced in Sec.~\ref{sec:fc}.
